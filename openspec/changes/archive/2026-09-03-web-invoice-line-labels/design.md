## Context

See proposal.md — Why. What the backend actually writes, which decides what can be derived:

| kind | stored description | who wrote it |
|---|---|---|
| `rent` | `Rent 2026-08` | generated, from the rent period |
| `electricity` | `Electricity 80 kWh` | generated, from the consumption |
| `water` | `Water, 2 occupant(s)` | generated, from the occupant count |
| `serviceFee` | the fee's catalogue name | **the owner** |
| `charge` | what the owner typed | **the owner** |
| `deposit` | one of four fixed phrases | generated, but not determined by kind alone |

Every line also carries `quantity`, `unitAmount`, `periodStart`, `periodEnd` and its `amount`.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**

- The charges on a bill read in Vietnamese.
- Nothing stored changes; old invoices read like new ones.
- No distinction the record makes is lost in the labelling.

**Non-Goals:**

- Changing the generator, or backfilling.
- The tenant portal.
- Any change to a figure.

## Decisions

**Three kinds are rebuilt from the row; two are shown as written.**

`rent`, `electricity` and `water` are fully described by their kind plus the data already on the line. Their stored text is a rendering of those same fields, so rebuilding it loses nothing — and the rebuild cannot drift from the figures, because it reads the figures.

`serviceFee` and `charge` are the opposite: their text is what a person wrote. A service fee's name comes from the owner's own catalogue, and an ad-hoc charge is a sentence the owner typed about a broken window. Translating either would be rewriting somebody's words, and most likely into the language they already used.

That split is the whole design. It is not "translate what we can" — it is that generated text is reproducible and human text is not ours to touch.

**The kind is a proxy for who wrote the text, and it has one exception.** Found while verifying, not while designing: cancelling a tenancy records what the owner keeps as an ad-hoc `charge`, because that is the machinery it reuses to reach the revenue report. So one system-written phrase arrives on a line whose kind otherwise means a person wrote it, and it appeared in English on a cancelled tenancy's bill.

It is matched by that exact phrase rather than by widening the rule to all charges — which would put every owner-written sentence back in scope for rewriting, and lose the distinction the design rests on.

**Deposit lines are matched against their four known phrases.**

This is the uncomfortable part, and it is uncomfortable in both directions.

A deposit line's meaning is not determined by its kind. The four phrases mean genuinely different things — money taken, money topped up, money handed back, money kept — and one of them carries a negative amount. Labelling all four "Tiền cọc" would put a negative figure on screen under a word that gives no account of why money is moving the other way.

Two ways to tell them apart:

- **Re-derive from signals** — negative means a return, an ad-hoc invoice means a cancellation, and so on. Rejected: that is a second copy of a rule the backend owns, in a place that cannot be kept in step with it, which is the failure this codebase has spent several changes eliminating.
- **Match the phrases.** Fragile in the ordinary sense — a reworded phrase stops matching — but the set is closed, generated by code in this repository, and the failure is graceful: an unmatched line falls through to its stored text, which is exactly today's behaviour. A wrong label is impossible; only a missed one.

The second is chosen because its worst case is the current state and the first's worst case is a confident lie.

**Matching is exact and anchored, not fuzzy.**

A prefix or substring match would eventually catch an owner-written charge that happens to begin with the same word. The deposit phrases are compared in full, and only for lines of kind `deposit` — a `charge` line saying "Deposit returned to Mr Nam by hand" is a sentence the owner wrote and stays untouched.

**The quantity and rate column is left alone.**

It already renders `80 × 3.500 ₫` from the numeric fields, which is language-neutral. Nothing to do.

## Risks / Trade-offs

**A deposit phrase is reworded in the backend and stops matching** → It falls through to the stored English, which is today's behaviour, and the miss is visible on screen to anyone who opens such an invoice. The alternative designs fail silently or fail wrongly.

**A rebuilt label disagrees with the stored description** → It cannot disagree about the figures, since it reads them. It can differ in wording from what a very old invoice stored, which is intended: the record keeps what was written, the screen shows what it means.

**The tenant portal still shows the stored English** → True, and named in the proposal as out of scope. It is a screen a tenant sees rather than the owner, and deserves its own decision.
